summary: "A repair tool and the repair it performed. Every ticket inside a body of related work demanded a build step the runner deliberately never performs for such tickets, so the runner finished the work and then refused to record it as finished. The tool corrects each ticket's own account of itself, refuses rather than guesses on any shape it does not recognise, and can be run again as more such tickets appear."
description: "A ticket that belongs to a larger body of work listed a final publishing step as required. The runner deliberately skips that step for such tickets, because one publication covers the whole batch, so the step never ran, never recorded an outcome, and the check that decides whether a ticket is finished refused to mark it done. The work was complete; the record said otherwise; the run stopped. Three hundred and thirty-two tickets were in that state across twenty-three bodies of work, and the run stopped on whichever finished first, so repairing one by hand only moved the stoppage to the next. This tool corrects the record rather than the runner: it marks the step explicitly excluded, which is what the runner actually does, and removes the corresponding row from the ticket's sign-off checklist so the two halves of the record agree. It never marks the step as completed — no such step ran, and recording one that did would place a false statement inside the very evidence the finished-check reads, which is the failure this whole area exists to prevent. It touches only tickets inside a body of work, because a standalone ticket genuinely does perform that step. It leaves already-finished tickets alone, because their records are no longer the runner's to change. It edits the two lines in place rather than reading and rewriting the file, so nothing else in the ticket moves by a single byte. And where it meets a shape it cannot account for it refuses, names the file, and ends the run unsuccessfully rather than making a half-change. Running it surfaced a ticket template nobody remembered: seventeen files carry the step in their heading block but have no sign-off checklist at all. Those were refused on the first pass, deliberately, and the question was escalated rather than decided by the tool. Checking the downstream validator settled it — with no checklist there is nothing for an excluded step to wrongly appear in, so correcting the heading block alone is the complete repair, and it removes a violation those files were already carrying rather than creating one. The tool now handles that shape and still refuses the genuinely ambiguous one, where a checklist exists but the expected row is missing. Applied, it changed three hundred and fifteen tickets and left seventeen finished ones untouched, with nothing refused. The change is verifiable without trusting the tool's own report: the recorded difference is three hundred and fifteen lines added and six hundred and thirteen removed, and the gap between those two numbers is exactly the number of sign-off rows that existed to be removed. A second run reports nothing left to do. It will need running again — more such tickets are still being produced until the generator that creates them is corrected."
